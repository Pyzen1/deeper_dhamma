The Audio Heritage & Mission

Preserving and opening access to thirty years of oral Dhamma

Founded in 1973 in Perth, the Buddhist Society of Western Australia (BSWA) has grown into one of the world's most vibrant centers for early Buddhist monastic training, meditation retreats, and public Dhamma teachings. Over three decades, the monastics of Bodhinyana Monastery, Dhammasara Nuns Monastery, and the Armadale Meditation Centre have offered thousands of Friday evening public talks, weekend Sutta study workshops, and 9-day meditation retreats at Jhana Grove.

Historically recorded onto analog cassettes, digital mini-discs, and MP3 files—and later distributed via the Deeper Dhamma podcast feed on Podbean—these teachings represent an irreplaceable treasure of practical meditation instruction, sutta analysis, and compassionate monastic wisdom.

Curated English Archive: The Deeper Dhamma collection indexes 3,246 English-language monastic recordings spanning over thirty years. Non-English talks (such as Sinhala, Thai, Chinese, and Vietnamese translations) and duplicate defunct feeds have been excluded to guarantee that search indexes, transcripts, and word alignments remain 100% focused on English oral Dhamma teachings.

The Deeper Dhamma Solution: This platform transforms the entire audio archive into an instant, searchable, studyable knowledge base. Audio streams 1:1 bit-for-bit from official Podbean servers, while every spoken word is mapped in real time to high-precision transcripts, canonical dictionaries, and authoritative source texts.

The Principle of Dāna (Free Offering)

"Sabbadānaṃ dhammadānaṃ jināti" — The gift of Dhamma excels all gifts. (Dhp 354)

In accordance with the monastic Vinaya and the living Buddhist tradition of generosity (dāna), the Dhamma teachings are priceless and freely accessible to everyone without paywalls, subscriptions, or advertisements. To help cover ongoing infrastructure costs, maintenance, and continuous open-source software development, voluntary donations (dāna) are gratefully welcomed to support the upkeep and future enhancement of this platform.

Technical Architecture & Data Integrity

How Deeper Dhamma achieves speed, privacy, and acoustic fidelity

Client-Side WASM

100% Serverless & Privacy-Focused

The entire database, full-text index, and query engine run directly in your web browser using official SQLite compiled to WebAssembly (WASM). Operating inside a dedicated Web Worker with Origin Private File System (OPFS) and IndexedDB caching, searches complete with zero server round-trips.

  • ✓ Privacy-focused: collects only anonymous usage telemetry and error diagnostics strictly for site improvements
  • ✓ No third-party ad networks, tracking pixels, or monetization of personal data
  • ✓ No account registration, passwords, or paywalls
  • ✓ Progressive Web App (PWA) offline shell caching via Service Worker
CUDA Float16 ASR

High-Accuracy Speech-to-Text

Transcriptions are generated using faster-whisper-large-v3 in float16 precision on NVIDIA CUDA, configured for sequential transcription (batch_size=1) with tuned Silero Voice Activity Detection (VAD).

  • ✓ 100% speech capture with zero dropped words or boundary truncation
  • ✓ Preserves quiet monastic speech, gentle laughs, and meditative pauses
  • ✓ Dynamic teacher prompt steering conditioned on canonical Pali vocabulary
ECAPA-TDNN

Era-Aware Voiceprints & Diarization

Speaker diarization utilizes 192-dimensional SpeechBrain ECAPA-TDNN embeddings cataloging monastics across historical eras (1994–2003, 2004–2014, 2015–present).

  • ✓ Distinguishes primary teachers, retreat coordinators, and audience Q&A
  • ✓ Hysteresis smoothing eliminates false speaker flips on solo monologues
  • ✓ Splits dialogue exchanges at natural conversational pauses (≥ 0.45s)
Pristine Bitstream

Bitstream Parity & Seek Calibration

Audio is streamed 1:1 bit-for-bit from Podbean's official CDN without re-encoding or container modification.

  • ✓ VBR header seek calibration eliminates cross-browser audio drift
  • ✓ Pinpoint seek accuracy in Chrome, Firefox, Safari, and iOS WebKit
  • ✓ Anti-drift terminology normalization guarantees canonical Pali spelling

Monastic Teachers Featured

Centuries of Theravada Forest Tradition lineage preserved in voice

☸

Ajahn Brahm (Ajahn Brahmavamso)

Abbot of Bodhinyana Monastery • Spiritual Director of the BSWA

Born in London, Ajahn Brahm studied theoretical physics at Cambridge University before ordaining in Thailand at age 23 under the renowned meditation master Venerable Ajahn Chah of the Thai Forest Tradition. For over forty years, he has guided Bodhinyana Monastery in Serpentine, WA. World-renowned for his lucid, deeply practical teachings on meditation, the four jhānas, unconditional forgiveness, and joyful letting go, his talks are characterized by profound wisdom, accessibility, and delightful humor.

📜

Ajahn Brahmali

Senior Resident Monk • Pali & Vinaya Scholar

Born in Norway, Ajahn Brahmali ordained in 1996 under Venerable Ajahn Chah and Ajahn Brahm. A resident teacher at Bodhinyana Monastery, he is one of the world's foremost scholars and English translators of the Vinaya Piṭaka (the Buddhist Monastic Code), completed for SuttaCentral. He regularly teaches comprehensive Sutta courses, Pali language workshops, and meditation retreats, making ancient canonical texts vivid and deeply relevant for contemporary practitioners.

🏛️

Bhante Sujato

Co-Founder of SuttaCentral • Early Buddhist Texts Scholar

Ordained in 1994 in Thailand under the Thai Forest Tradition, Bhante Sujato is former Abbot of Santi Forest Monastery and co-founder of SuttaCentral. He is celebrated for his historic complete English translation of the four Pali Nikāyas (Dīgha, Majjhima, Saṃyutta, and Aṅguttara), rendering canonical scriptures into plain, clear, gender-inclusive English freely available in the public domain worldwide.

🪷

Ayya Vayama & BSWA Sangha

Pioneer Theravada Bhikkhunī • Monastic Sangha

Ayya Vayama (1952–2021) was an Australian Buddhist nun who was instrumental in founding Dhammasara Nuns Monastery in Gidgegannup, WA—the first forest monastery for women in the Southern Hemisphere. Her talks, along with teachings by visiting monastics and Sangha members across 30 years, are preserved here for future generations.
